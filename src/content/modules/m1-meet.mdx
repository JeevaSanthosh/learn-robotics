A <Term id="robot">robot</Term> is any machine that does three things, over and over: it **senses**, it **decides**, and it **acts**.

Don't take that on trust — step through it. Drag the wall closer and further, and watch what changes at each stage.

That third slider is the interesting one. **You** chose where the stop line goes. The robot doesn't know what a wall is, or what "too close" means — it knows one number and one comparison, and you decided what the comparison should be.

That is the actual job. Not building the machine — deciding what it should do with what it sees.

## The one you'll be programming

Your simulated robot has all three parts:

- A <Term id="sensor">sensor</Term> on its nose that measures distance to walls — that's **sense**.
- A tiny computer brain that runs *your* program — that's **decide**.
- Two wheels and an <Term id="led">LED</Term> — its <Term id="actuator">actuators</Term>, the parts that actually *do* things — that's **act**.

Everything else in this course is getting better at those three steps. Sharper senses, better decisions, more precise actions — but always that loop, always in that order.

**Next lesson:** you make the LED blink. It's the "hello!" of every robot ever built, and it's the *act* stage on its own — no sensing, no deciding, just one part moving because you said so.
