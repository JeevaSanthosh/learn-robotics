Here's the mission every robot vacuum, drone, and self-driving car starts with: **approach the wall, stop before touching it.**

The catch: you could just count squares and move exactly that far. But that's *you* deciding, not the robot — move the wall and your program breaks. So this mission has rules:

1. Get within about **1 square** of the wall.
2. **No touching.** A bump fails the run.
3. The **sensor block must make the decision** — the mission won't count without it.

The cleanest tool is the **repeat until** block: *repeat until distance ≤ 1 → move a little.* Little steps, checking the sensor between each one.

When it works, move an imaginary wall in your head: would your program still work if the wall were closer? Farther? *Yes* — because the robot is sensing, not memorizing. That's the difference between a program and a wind-up toy.

## Under the hood: what the sensor can't see

Your program compares a reading against a number — that number is the <Term id="threshold">threshold</Term>. Choosing it well is most of the skill, and it depends on things the sensor never tells you:

- **Stopping distance.** The robot keeps moving after you decide to stop. Faster robot, bigger threshold.
- **Check rate.** Between two checks the robot is blind. This loop is <Term id="polling">polling</Term>: read, decide, move, repeat. Move too far per pass and you can sail straight past your own threshold.

- **Angle.** A distance sensor measures along one narrow line. A wall approached at a sharp angle reflects the signal *away* instead of back, so the reading stays large right up until the crash.


The angle problem is why real robots carry several sensors pointing different ways. A single sensor doesn't give you "the distance to the wall" — it gives you "the distance along this one line, if something reflects".

## Bonus: don't crash — six worlds, one program

Counting squares works right up until the wall moves. Here's the real test of a robot: **the same program has to work when the world changes.** This mission grades your program across **six worlds** where the wall sits at a different distance each time. Pass at least five without crashing.

A memorised "move forward N" can't win this — it'll crash where the wall is closer and stop short where it's farther. Only *sensing* survives all six.
